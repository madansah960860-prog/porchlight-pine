About this one

Two gallons is about as much water as most people want to carry in one hand, and this can is built around that. The body is hot-dipped galvanised steel — dipped, not sprayed, so the coating is in the seams as well as on the face — with a rolled rim and a soldered spout seam.

There are two handles, which is the part that matters. The top bail carries it, and the rear strap handle lets you tip it with your wrist instead of your shoulder. The spout is 13 inches long so you can reach the back of a bed or the base of a plant under foliage.

A removable brass rose screws onto the spout end for a fine spread over seedlings; take it off and you get a single stream for watering at the root. Galvanised steel will develop a soft grey patina — that is the zinc doing its job, not a fault.

Care
  • Empty it after use and store it upside down or on its side so water does not stand in the base.
  • Empty completely before a hard freeze; water expanding in a full can will split a seam.
  • Expect a grey patina over time. Do not scour it back to bright metal — that removes the zinc.
  • Rinse the brass rose if the holes clog with mineral scale.
