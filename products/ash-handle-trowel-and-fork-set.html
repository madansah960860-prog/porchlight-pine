About this one

A hand trowel gets more use than any other tool in a garden, which is why it is worth owning one that is not stamped out of thin steel. These have carbon steel heads with a rolled edge, set on a tang that runs into the handle and is fixed with a brass rivet — not a socket crimped around the wood, which is the joint that fails first.

The handles are turned ash, 4.75 inches long with a swell at the end so your hand has something to pull against. Ash is the traditional choice for tool handles because it takes a shock without splitting. They come bare, which is how we prefer them; a wipe of linseed oil once a year is all they want.

The trowel blade is 6 inches long and dished for scooping; the fork has three flat tines for lifting clumps and teasing out roots. Both have a hanging hole through the handle end.

Care
  • Rinse the soil off and dry the heads after each use — carbon steel will rust if left wet.
  • Wipe the heads with a lightly oiled rag before putting them away for the winter.
  • Rub the ash handles with boiled linseed oil once a season.
  • Not dishwasher safe.
