About this one

Unglazed terracotta is porous, and that is the feature rather than the flaw. The clay wall breathes, which means roots get air and a heavy watering does not sit around the crown of the plant. Geraniums, pelargoniums, herbs and anything else that hates wet feet do well in it.

This is a 12-inch pot measured across the top, 11 inches tall, with a rolled rim you can actually grip when it is full and wet — the detail that makes a pot liftable. There is one 0.75-inch drainage hole in the base and a matching terracotta saucer in the box.

Terracotta is fired clay, so it is breakable and it is not frost-proof. In a climate that freezes, empty it and bring it under cover before winter. We would rather tell you that now than replace it in March.

Care
  • Soak a new pot in water for an hour before first planting so the dry clay does not pull moisture out of your compost.
  • Scrub white mineral bloom off with a stiff brush and water, or leave it — most people like it.
  • Empty and store under cover before a hard freeze. Terracotta is not frost-proof.
  • Do not put it in a dishwasher or use bleach on it.
