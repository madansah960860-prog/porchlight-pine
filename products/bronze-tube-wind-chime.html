About this one

Most of what makes a chime pleasant rather than clattery is the tuning, and most cheap chimes are not tuned at all. The six tubes here are cut to a pentatonic scale, which is the scale with no semitones in it — the practical effect is that no two tubes can ring at once and sound wrong, whatever order the wind hits them in.

Tubes are 1-inch aluminium with a bronze powder-coat, strung on braided polyester cord through a hardwood top disc. Aluminium is deliberate: it rings longer than steel and it will not rust streaks onto a porch ceiling. The striker is a solid hardwood clapper and the sail below it is a turned wooden teardrop.

Overall drop is 24 inches from the hanging ring to the bottom of the sail. Hang it where it gets a little wind, not a lot; a chime in a gale is nobody's friend.

Care
  • Rinse the tubes with plain water and dry them if they get dusty or pollen-covered.
  • Check the cord at the top disc once a year and replace it if it looks chafed.
  • Bring it in over winter in a climate with ice storms — ice on the sail will stretch the cord.
