About this one

A bamboo chime sounds nothing like a metal one. It is a hollow wooden knock — closer to a xylophone or a set of woodblocks — and it carries much less far, which some people very much prefer on a small porch or near a bedroom window.

Eight tubes of kiln-dried bamboo hang from a carved wooden crown on waxed cotton cord. Kiln drying matters here: green bamboo splits along the grain within a season, dried bamboo does not. Each tube is cut through one node so it is closed at one end, which is what gives it a pitch instead of a rattle.

It is 30 inches overall, so it wants a little height. Under a porch roof or a deep eave is ideal — bamboo is happiest out of standing rain.

Care
  • Hang it under cover if you can; bamboo lasts far longer out of standing rain.
  • Wipe with a dry cloth. Do not soak it or put it under a hose.
  • A wipe of clear penetrating oil once a year keeps the colour from going grey.
  • Bring it indoors for the winter in a wet climate.
