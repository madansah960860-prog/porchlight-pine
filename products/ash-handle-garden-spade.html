About this one

A border spade is the tool you want if you are edging beds, lifting perennials, or digging in a space too tight for a full-size digging spade. The head on this one is 7 inches across and 11 inches down — narrower than a builder's spade, which is exactly the point.

The head is forged carbon steel with a rolled shoulder that forms a flat tread, so you can put a boot on it without standing on a sharp edge. It is riveted to a solid ash shaft that ends in a turned Y-handle. Overall height is 38 inches, which suits most people digging from a standing position; if you are much over six feet you may prefer a longer T-handle model, and we would rather say so than sell you the wrong one.

The blade arrives with a working edge, not a razor edge. A few passes with a file down the front face will sharpen it, and a sharp spade is much less effort than a blunt one.

Care
  • Scrape the soil off and dry the head after each use.
  • Wipe the steel with an oiled rag before winter storage.
  • Oil the ash shaft with boiled linseed oil once a season.
  • Sharpen the front face of the blade with a flat file; leave the back face flat.
