About this one

Water brings more birds to a yard than seed does, and it brings the ones that do not eat seed at all. The thing that makes a bath actually get used is depth: this basin is 2 inches at the centre and shallower at the rim, with a bush-hammered floor that gives wet feet something to grip.

It is cast stone in two pieces — basin and pedestal — which key together with a moulded collar. Set the pedestal on a paving slab or tamped gravel rather than bare soil, level it, then lower the basin on. Assembled it weighs 41 pounds, which is enough that it will not go over in a storm and not so much that two people cannot move it.

In freezing climates, tip the basin out and store it under cover for the winter; water left to freeze in any cast stone basin will eventually crack it. We say so plainly rather than pretend otherwise.

Care
  • Tip out and scrub with a stiff brush every 3 to 4 days in warm weather to keep it clean.
  • Use plain water only, or a very dilute white-vinegar rinse followed by a thorough rinse.
  • Never use bleach or detergent in a bird bath.
  • Empty and store the basin under cover before the first hard freeze.
